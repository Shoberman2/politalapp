## Problem Statement

BallotWatch can explain how members voted in the past, but it does not answer the question users naturally ask before a scheduled floor vote: **How is each member likely to vote, and what does that imply for the chamber tally?**

The feature must publish probabilities without pretending to know motives or treating a forecast as fact. It must also prove that every input was available before the vote. A model that uses the completed roll call's party majority, final tally, post-vote actions, or later bill metadata is not a forecast.

## What Makes This Cool

An officially scheduled bill gains a living forecast page:

- Every active member receives a Yea probability and, only when confidence clears a validated threshold, a likely Yea or likely Nay call.
- The page aggregates member probabilities into an estimated chamber tally.
- Low-confidence members remain **Too close to call** instead of being forced into a side.
- After the real roll call, the forecast freezes permanently and becomes a predicted-versus-actual record.
- The most interesting output is not a generic accuracy badge. It is the list of members who broke a strong historical pattern, with evidence available before the vote.

The forecast is useful before the vote and accountable afterward.

## Constraints

- React 18 + Vite SPA with Supabase, TypeScript/JavaScript ETL, and GitHub Actions.
- Visual treatment must follow `DESIGN.md`: editorial reading order, restrained color, thin rules, typography-led hierarchy, no dashboard gauges or good/bad party coloring.
- Forecasts are published only for a bill and vote target named by an official House or Senate source.
- Existing source certainty (`scheduled` or `tentative`) is preserved verbatim. Tentative Senate attention is never presented as a guaranteed vote.
- The forecast engine cannot use an LLM to choose a member's vote. AI may narrate already-computed evidence, but the first release does not require narration.
- Official CRS summaries or official bill text are the only text inputs. AI-generated summaries are excluded from training and inference.
- Predictions, inputs, model version, and cutoff timestamp are immutable after publication.
- House and Senate models activate independently. A chamber that fails its release gate stays in shadow mode.
- Existing deployment remains Vercel. Official-source polling continues through the ten-minute bill-alert GitHub Actions workflow.

## Research Evidence

- Earlier roll-call work reported 90.6% overall accuracy against an 84.5% majority baseline, while minority-class F1 was only 0.645. Overall accuracy alone hides the votes users care about most.
- Models combining legislative text and relational context outperform text-only or graph-only models.
- More realistic time-based splits reduce performance because they introduce new members and new bills. Random folds are not a release-quality evaluation.
- LLM political-agent systems are promising, but cost, nondeterminism, calibration, and political-bias risk make them a challenger model rather than the trusted first release.

## Premises

1. Forecast only bills and vote targets named by official floor sources.
2. Historical vote backfill is Phase 0. `etl/backfillVotes.ts` is currently a scaffold and cannot train a production model.
3. Reuse the Pattern Match product surface and historical features, not its formula. `computePatternMatchScore()` uses the completed roll call's party majority as a 60% signal, which is post-vote leakage.
4. Freeze every feature as of `input_cutoff_at`. No final roll-call statistics, post-vote actions, or later metadata may enter an example.
5. Evaluate with rolling chronological splits and compare against party-only, member-history, and caucus-history baselines.
6. Measure Brier score, calibration, crossover/minority F1, called-vote precision, coverage, and chamber-tally error. Overall accuracy is secondary.
7. Permit no-call output. A calibrated probability near 50% is uncertainty, not a prediction failure.
8. Store immutable forecast snapshots and compare them with actual votes in a separate evaluation record.
9. Code computes probabilities. Narrative code may describe evidence but cannot speculate about motives.

## Approaches Considered

- **Transparent direct model:** One regularized member-bill classifier. It remains a required Phase B candidate and production fallback because it may calibrate better on limited historical data.
- **Two-stage caucus and crossover model:** Chosen as the primary candidate. It mirrors the structure of congressional voting and makes party-direction errors distinct from member-crossover errors.
- **LLM political-agent ensemble:** Deferred as a benchmark challenger. It is less reproducible, harder to calibrate, more expensive, and more exposed to invented political reasoning.

The selected architecture is a hypothesis, not permission to ignore evidence. Phase B trains both the direct and two-stage candidates against identical frozen splits. The simplest candidate that passes every gate ships. If the direct model wins, the caucus/crossover decomposition remains a diagnostic report rather than the production scorer.

## Recommended Approach

Build a time-censored, probabilistic forecasting pipeline with three components:

```text
official floor source
        │
        ▼
canonical bill_event + parsed vote target
        │
        ▼
time-frozen bill/member feature snapshot
        │
        ├── participation estimate: will this member cast a substantive vote?
        ├── caucus model: what direction will each caucus take?
        └── crossover model: how likely is this member to depart from that direction?
        │
        ▼
calibrated member P(Yea) + Yea/Nay/no-call
        │
        ▼
expected chamber tally + conditional passage probability
        │
        ▼
immutable database snapshot → BillDetail forecast → post-vote evaluation
```

### Forecast target

Do not forecast an unspecified concept of “support for the bill.” A scheduled item must be parsed into a target:

- `final_passage`
- `suspend_and_pass`
- `cloture`
- `motion_to_proceed`
- `other_procedural`
- `unknown`

Version one publicly supports `final_passage` and `suspend_and_pass`. `cloture` and `motion_to_proceed` remain in shadow evaluation until they have enough target-matched history. `unknown` never publishes a member forecast.

The UI shows passage probability only when the target and vote threshold are known. Otherwise it shows an estimated Yea/Nay distribution with no claim about passage.

### Phase 0: provenance spike and historical data

Before a multi-Congress backfill, create `docs/methodology/forecast-data-provenance.md`. For every proposed feature, record its official source, earliest trustworthy timestamp, historical revision availability, stable identifier, retention window, and whether an as-of value can be reconstructed. A feature with no provable pre-vote timestamp is excluded from version one. “Timestamp unavailable” is not interpreted as “known before the vote.”

Then implement `etl/backfillVotes.ts` for the 115th through 118th Congresses. The backfill must:

1. Parameterize existing House and Senate vote extraction by Congress and session.
2. Insert historical member records and `member_congress_terms` before inserting votes.
3. Preserve the member's party, state, district, caucus, and term boundaries as of each roll call.
4. Add and backfill normalized target fields on `roll_calls`: raw question text, `forecast_target`, target parser version, threshold rule, parse confidence, and unsupported reason.
5. Backfill only official bill metadata whose historical as-of state passes the provenance spike. Sponsor identity is safe from introduction; cosponsors use `cosponsored_at`; mutable summaries, routings, and status fields are excluded unless their revision timestamp is preserved.
6. Resume through `backfill_state`, use bounded request budgets, and never delete current data.
7. Run existing attribution and tally sanity checks, including caucus overrides and duplicate-seat detection.

Initial training scope is the 115th through 117th Congresses with the 118th as an untouched final test. After the release gate is fixed, retrain on the 115th through 118th Congresses for 119th-Congress production. Rolling-origin validation inside the training range selects hyperparameters.

### Time-censored feature builder

Create one training row per `(roll_call_id, politician_id)` with `feature_cutoff_at` equal to the official schedule publication time when known. If a historical schedule timestamp cannot be reconstructed, use a documented conservative cutoff before `voted_at`, mark `cutoff_quality='synthetic_conservative'`, and keep that row out of the untouched release-gate test. Synthetic cutoffs may be used only for exploratory training.

Persist every canonical row in an immutable feature snapshot before training or inference. The snapshot stores the ordered numeric/categorical/text inputs, missingness flags, source row/revision identifiers, cutoff quality, feature schema version, canonical JSON, and SHA-256. Current mutable bill rows are never sufficient proof of point-in-time availability.

Allowed features:

- Bill: official title tokens, policy area, bill type, chamber, supported vote target, sponsor party, and only those cosponsor counts/party shares whose `cosponsored_at` is at or before cutoff.
- Caucus: prior target-matched votes on the same policy area, sponsor-party relationship, chamber majority status, and historical caucus cohesion calculated only from earlier roll calls.
- Member: prior policy-area Yea rate, prior crossover rate, recency-weighted participation, effective caucus, tenure, and prior target-matched votes.
- Context: session, days remaining in Congress, schedule certainty, and known vote threshold.

District lean, Voteview ideal points, committee routing, related-measure history, donor alignment, and CRS-summary text enter only later ablations after the provenance spike demonstrates point-in-time reconstruction and the minimal candidates establish a trusted baseline.

Forbidden features:

- The current roll call's `roll_call_stats`, final tally, winning side, or actual party majority.
- Actions, cosponsors, amendments, summaries, or status changes recorded after cutoff.
- AI-generated bill explanations.
- News sentiment, social-media speculation, private whip counts, or unsupported claims about motive.
- The member's actual vote on the target or any duplicate/copy of the same roll call in another split.

Add a leakage assertion that fails dataset generation if any source timestamp exceeds the row cutoff, any required timestamp is absent, the same event family crosses train/test boundaries, or the canonical snapshot hash changes without a feature-schema bump.

### Model training and artifact contract

Training runs offline in Python from a locked `ml/pyproject.toml` and `ml/uv.lock`. Pin Python 3.12, `scikit-learn`, NumPy, and every transitive dependency. Inference runs in TypeScript inside a separate forecast worker using a canonical JSON artifact, so the ten-minute bill-alert workflow does not install Python.

Version one deliberately narrows the cross-runtime pipeline:

- Unicode normalize text to NFKC, lowercase, and tokenize with the explicit regex `[a-z][a-z0-9'-]+`.
- Use unigrams only, an exported lexicographically ordered vocabulary, exported IDF values, and L2-normalized TF-IDF.
- Encode categorical values with exported sorted vocabularies. Unknown values map to all-zero plus an explicit unknown indicator.
- Impute numeric values to the exported training median, add missing indicators, then standardize with exported means and population standard deviations.
- Train L2 logistic regressions with `solver='lbfgs'`, `max_iter=2000`, `tol=1e-10`, `random_state=0`, and float64. The crossover candidate uses balanced sample weights; every candidate is calibrated afterward.
- Fit Platt calibration parameters on the last chronological validation fold, not on final test data. Export the sigmoid slope and intercept directly.
- Serialize RFC-8785-style canonical UTF-8 JSON with sorted object keys and no insignificant whitespace. The artifact JSON contains no self-hash. `forecast_models.artifact_sha256` is the sole checksum and covers every stored byte in `artifact_bytes`.

The artifact contains semantic version, artifact schema version, feature order, preprocessing rules, vocabularies, IDF values, imputation/scaling values, direct/caucus/crossover coefficients, Platt parameters, dataset and snapshot fingerprints, training Congresses, final cutoff, per-deployment call thresholds, evaluation report hash, and golden fixtures.

Python and TypeScript inference must match golden vectors within `1e-6` absolute probability tolerance. An unknown schema version, checksum mismatch, missing feature, non-finite coefficient, or fixture mismatch makes the deployment unloadable.

#### Participation estimate

Version one uses an executable recency-weighted estimate. For each eligible member, consider every earlier roll call in the same chamber with `voted_at < feature_cutoff_at`. For prior vote `i`, let `w_i = 2^(-age_days_i / 180)` and `y_i = 1` for Yea or Nay and `0` for Present or Not Voting. Compute the chamber prior with Beta(1,1) smoothing, `p_chamber = (Σw_i y_i + 1) / (Σw_i + 2)`, over all previously eligible members. Then compute `p_participates = (Σ_member w_i y_i + 20 × p_chamber) / (Σ_member w_i + 20)`. A member with no prior history receives `p_chamber`; vacancies and members outside their `member_congress_terms` window receive zero. The same formula is normative in Python and TypeScript fixtures.

#### Direct model

The direct candidate predicts `q_yea = P(Yea | participates)` for each member from the frozen bill, caucus, and member features. It produces the same member probabilities and expected tally fields as the two-stage candidate. Version one leaves `passage_probability` null for a direct-model deployment because that model does not define the shared caucus latent variables required by the joint simulator.

#### Caucus model

For each chamber and effective caucus, predict `P(caucus majority = Yea)` using only bill and pre-vote caucus features. This replaces the leaked completed-roll-call majority in the descriptive Pattern Match formula.

Effective caucus is resolved from `member_congress_terms` as of the vote. An independent who formally caucuses with a party uses that caucus. An unaffiliated independent or a one-member caucus receives no caucus-majority label; the two-stage candidate backs off to the member-history baseline and displays Uncalled when sparse. For that baseline, `q_unaffiliated` is the Beta(1,1)-smoothed Yea rate among all earlier members in the same `(chamber, target, policy_area)`; with fewer than 10 examples it falls back to the chamber-target Yea rate. The member's own earlier substantive votes are shrunk toward `q_unaffiliated` with weight 20. Actual caucus-majority ties are excluded from caucus and crossover training, validation, and F1 evaluation. Votes cast before a member changed caucus may remain provenance-cleared history features, but they do not count toward the current-caucus sparse-history threshold.

#### Crossover model

Predict `P(member follows caucus)` conditional on the predicted caucus direction. Version-one inputs emphasize the member's prior policy-area crossover history, tenure, participation, and relationship to the sponsor coalition.

For each member, keep conditional vote direction separate from participation:

```text
q_yea = P(Yea | participates) = [
  P(caucus=Yea) × P(follows caucus)
  + P(caucus=Nay) × P(crosses caucus)
]

p_yea        = P(participates) × q_yea
p_nay        = P(participates) × (1 - q_yea)
p_not_voting = 1 - P(participates)
```

The three unconditional probabilities must each be in `[0,1]` and sum to 1 within `1e-9`.

### Calibration and calls

- Calibrate each `(chamber, forecast_target, candidate_model)` separately using only chronological validation predictions.
- Select call thresholds before final test evaluation. Thresholds must meet the release precision target and then maximize coverage.
- Base Yea/Nay calls on `q_yea`, never unconditional `p_yea`. Require `P(participates) ≥ 0.65`; below that threshold the call is Uncalled regardless of direction.
- Suggested initial display bands, subject to validation: likely Yea at `q_yea ≥ 0.67`, likely Nay at `q_yea ≤ 0.33`, otherwise Too close to call.
- Never lower thresholds merely to fill the chamber map.
- A member with fewer than 20 earlier substantive roll calls under the current effective caucus uses the predicted caucus probability as `q_yea` and is always displayed Uncalled. The probabilities still contribute to expected counts. A member without a usable caucus uses the member-history baseline and is also displayed Uncalled while sparse.

### Tally and passage probability

Expected counts are `Σp_yea`, `Σp_nay`, and `Σp_not_voting` over the eligible roster for either candidate. Only a two-stage deployment computes passage probability, using 20,000 deterministic-seed Monte Carlo draws from the joint hierarchy rather than independent member coin flips:

1. Draw one shared caucus direction per caucus from its calibrated probability.
2. Draw each member's participation.
3. Conditional on participation and the shared caucus draw, draw whether the member follows or crosses.
4. For an unaffiliated member with no shared caucus, draw Yea independently from that member's fallback `q_yea`, conditional on participation.
5. Apply the target's versioned threshold rule.

Threshold rules are functions, not fixed integers: simple majority of members present and voting for final passage; two-thirds of members present and voting for suspension; three-fifths of the duly chosen and sworn Senate for cloture unless an official rule indicates otherwise. Ties and vice-presidential tie-breaking are represented explicitly. If the target parser cannot choose a threshold rule, `passage_probability` remains null.

### Evaluation and release gates

Create a machine-readable report matching `ml/schemas/evaluation-report.schema.json` and a human-readable methodology report. Every gate and activation decision is keyed by `(chamber, forecast_target)`.

Metric contract:

- Labels include substantive Yea and Nay only for direction metrics. Present/Not Voting are evaluated through participation Brier score and excluded from direction denominators.
- Direction Brier score is the mean `(q_yea - y)^2` among participating members. Participation Brier is reported separately.
- Relative Brier improvement is `(baseline_brier - candidate_brier) / baseline_brier`.
- ECE uses ten fixed bins `[0,.1), ... [.9,1]`, weighted by bin count. Report no slice with fewer than 100 member-votes; mark it insufficient instead of passing it.
- Called-vote precision is correct Yea/Nay calls divided by all Yea/Nay calls. Coverage is Yea/Nay calls divided by participating member-votes.
- Crossover is the positive class when the member differs from the actual caucus majority. Ties in actual caucus majority are excluded. F1 uses the standard harmonic mean and is also reported by policy area when that slice has at least 100 examples.
- Tally error is mean absolute difference between expected and actual Yea and Nay counts, averaged per roll call.
- Final-test uncertainty uses a roll-call-clustered bootstrap with 1,000 fixed-seed samples. A gate passes only when the 95% confidence interval also satisfies the stated bound.

Executable baselines live in `ml/baselines.py` and share golden fixtures with TypeScript. Every count is restricted to substantive Yea/Nay votes with `voted_at < feature_cutoff_at` and uses Beta(1,1) smoothing:

- **Party-only:** for the member's effective caucus and `(chamber, target)`, `q = (prior caucus Yea + 1) / (prior caucus substantive + 2)`. If empty, use the chamber-target Yea rate.
- **Caucus-history:** apply the same rate within `(chamber, target, policy_area, effective_caucus)`. With fewer than 10 earlier examples, fall back to party-only.
- **Member-history:** within `(chamber, target, policy_area)`, shrink the member rate toward caucus-history with weight 20: `q = (member Yea + 20 × q_caucus_history) / (member substantive + 20)`. With no earlier member examples, this equals caucus-history.

The release comparison uses these exact predictions, the same participation estimate, the same time splits, and the same metric implementation as the candidates.

Crossover evaluation is also executable for every candidate and baseline. The label is `1` only when a participating member differs from the actual, non-tied majority of the member's effective caucus. The two-stage candidate exports `r_cross = P(crosses caucus | participates)` from its joint model. For the direct candidate and each baseline, let `c` be the frozen caucus-history baseline probability that the caucus majority is Yea and let `q` be that scorer's member Yea probability; use `r_cross = c × (1 - q) + (1 - c) × q`. Each scorer selects one crossover-call threshold on chronological validation data to maximize F1, freezes it before the untouched test, and predicts crossover when `r_cross` meets that threshold. No actual caucus direction enters a prediction. The release gate compares candidate F1 with the better baseline using a paired roll-call-clustered bootstrap of the F1 difference; its lower 95% confidence bound must exceed zero.

A deployment activates only if its untouched chronological test set meets all gates:

1. Brier score improves at least 5% relative to the best simple baseline.
2. Expected calibration error is at most 0.05 overall and at most 0.08 within each major party slice.
3. Called-vote precision is at least 90% at 60% or greater coverage.
4. Crossover F1 exceeds both party-only and member-history baselines; the lower 95% confidence bound must be above the better baseline.
5. Estimated tally mean absolute error is at most 12 seats in the House and 4 seats in the Senate.
6. No leakage assertion, member-attribution sanity check, or Python/TypeScript parity fixture fails.

If one `(chamber, forecast_target)` deployment fails, other combinations may ship. Invalid combinations never create deployments. Failed targets remain shadow-only and continue collecting immutable live forecasts.

Forecast, snapshot, parse, supersession, and invalidation tables are append-only behind RPC-only service-role mutations; direct `UPDATE` and `DELETE` are revoked. Public visibility depends on the forecast's immutable `visibility='published'`, not whether its model is still active, so historical accountability survives model retirement. The current public read model additionally excludes superseded and invalidated forecasts. Shadow rows remain service-role-only. Add indexes for `(bill_id, visibility, generated_at DESC)`, job state/next attempt, `(source_event_id, deployment_id)`, deployment activation keys, and evaluation `roll_call_id`.

Local Postgres integration tests must prove RLS, artifact privacy, append-only enforcement, activation uniqueness, transaction rollback, idempotency, probability/count checks, and supersession visibility.

### Official-source integration

The existing bill-alert pipeline currently centers ingestion around followed bills. Forecasting needs every matched official floor item, not only items followed by a user.

Refactor source polling into two concerns:

1. **Canonical source ingestion:** persist all parseable official House and Senate floor items into `bill_source_items`, canonical `bill_events`, and correction chains.
2. **User fanout:** continue filtering notifications by `bill_follows` and preferences.

After canonical ingestion, enqueue forecast jobs without blocking notification fanout. A separate `runForecastJobs()` worker loads one artifact per deployment per run, creates and persists the immutable feature snapshot, runs batched TypeScript inference, and inserts header/member rows in one transaction. N+1 member-history queries are forbidden; load the roster and precomputed prior-vote aggregates in bounded bulk queries.

Artifact load or inference failure marks only the forecast job failed. Ordinary source ingestion and user alerts continue. Retry three times with exponential backoff, then dead-letter with a sanitized error code. A scheduled monitor alerts on dead-letter jobs or jobs leased longer than 20 minutes, and a guarded replay RPC allows manual retry after repair.

### Event-to-roll-call matcher

Evaluation uses a versioned deterministic matcher only after `roll_calls.member_votes_complete_at` is non-null. Candidate roll calls must match chamber, canonical bill, normalized forecast target under the pinned parser versions, and event series, and fall between 24 hours before the scheduled window and seven days after it. Cancellation events append `forecast_invalidations` and mark the evaluation cancelled. Rescheduled events match only their latest non-superseded series member.

Exactly one candidate produces `matched`. Zero candidates remain pending until the seven-day window expires, then move to `manual_review`. Multiple candidates produce `ambiguous`; they are never auto-scored. Repeat suspension attempts and multiple same-day bill votes require distinct normalized target text or manual review. The matcher persists every candidate ID and rule result in `match_evidence`.

Evaluation metrics are stored separately from prediction rows. No actual vote is written into a forecast or feature snapshot.

### Lifecycle state machines

```text
deployment: shadow → active → retired
forecast job: pending → running → succeeded
                    └→ failed_retryable → pending (max 3) → dead_letter
forecast: append shadow OR append published; never changes
correction: append successor forecast + append forecast_supersessions row
cancellation: append forecast_invalidations row; no successor required
evaluation: no row while pending → matched | ambiguous | cancelled | manual_review
```

Transaction ownership: canonical ingestion owns source events; the forecast worker owns snapshots, jobs, and forecasts; the evaluator owns matches and evaluations; activation RPCs own deployment changes.

## Engineering Review Addendum

### Scope challenge

The complete feature spans more than eight files and more than two new services. Implementing historical extraction, model training, live scheduling, forecast persistence, and UI in one pass would cross the design's own leakage gate. Scope is therefore split:

- **A0, build now:** provenance go/no-go, versioned target parser, forecast-foundation migration, atomic roll-call persistence, fenced checkpoint contract, canonical feature primitives, and a real local Supabase/Postgres test harness.
- **A1, after A0 gates pass:** 115th–118th Congress historical member intervals and roll-call backfill, immutable dataset snapshots, coverage/leakage report, and dataset manifest.
- **B–D:** remain ordered exactly as designed. No model, live forecast, or public UI begins early.

This is a sequencing reduction, not a product reduction. A0 exists because the current repository cannot yet prove the temporal facts A1 needs.

### What already exists

- `etl/backfillHistorical.ts` already provides the long-running Congress loop and error-budget vocabulary. Reuse its orchestration concepts, but replace its read/check sentinel with the fenced `etl_leases` RPCs already used by bill alerts.
- `etl/extractHouseVotes.ts` already parses current House API results and Senate XML, including en-bloc votes and surname-collision repairs. Extract pure parsers and identity helpers; do not run historical records through its current-only date assumptions.
- `etl/sources/congressGovMembers.ts` already writes `member_congress_terms`. Keep its paginated Congress lookup as one input, but do not treat Congress-start rows with null end dates as exact historical intervals.
- `etl/transform.ts` and `etl/load.ts` already normalize and load daily votes. Reuse pure normalization only. Do not reuse the generic loader for historical records because it overwrites current politician and bill fields.
- `roll_calls`, `votes`, `member_congress_terms`, `backfill_state`, `etl_leases`, target bill metadata, Vitest, Playwright, and the Supabase CLI are already present.
- Existing regression coverage for Senate menu parsing, member resolution, vote deduplication, load ordering, and migration contracts becomes required input rather than duplicated tests.

### Architecture findings and decisions

1. **[P0] Atomic completeness (confidence 10/10).** `etl/load.ts:93-117` performs separate HTTP writes and `etl/load.ts:395-418` batches votes independently. Decision: add a service-role-only `persist_historical_roll_call(...)` Postgres RPC. One transaction inserts or verifies the roll call, target parse, complete member-vote set, source tallies, and feature-independent provenance; it rejects conflicting prior votes, duplicate members, invalid positions, unresolved attribution, and tally mismatch before setting `member_votes_complete_at`.
2. **[P0] Historical-safe writes (confidence 10/10).** `etl/transform.ts:88` creates a current politician from every member vote, while `etl/load.ts:186-203` and `:259-287` update current politician and bill snapshots. Decision: historical adapters never call `loadToSupabase`. They insert missing politician identity shells with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, store temporal facts only in validated term intervals, link only provenance-whitelisted bills, and use the atomic roll-call RPC.
3. **[P0] Exact membership intervals (confidence 10/10).** `etl/sources/congressGovMembers.ts:159-170` currently assigns every member the Congress start and leaves caucus/end fields null. Decision: A0's provenance matrix must name an authoritative source for exact appointments, resignations, deaths, party changes, and caucus changes. A1 cannot start until the 118th-Congress roster can be reconstructed for every roll-call date and every interval has source evidence. Missing politician identities are inserted before terms; the existing trigger only updates identities that already exist.
4. **[P0] Fenced resumability (confidence 10/10).** `etl/backfillHistorical.ts:359-367` uses a non-atomic sentinel and `:397-415` resumes only by Congress. Decision: acquire `etl_leases` with a fence token, add a JSONB roll-call cursor to `backfill_state`, and advance it only inside/after the successful roll-call transaction. Status-only writes preserve every cursor field. `--resume` never bypasses the lease.
5. **[P1] Historical cutoff coverage (confidence 9/10).** Current `roll_calls` has vote date but no historical schedule-publication record. Decision: A0 sets a go/no-go threshold of at least 95% exact official cutoff coverage across supported 118th-Congress roll calls and 100% coverage for the untouched final-test rows actually scored. Anything else is `synthetic_conservative`, exploratory-only, and excluded from release gates. The archived official source and retention policy must be documented before bulk snapshots.
6. **[P1] Snapshot normalization (confidence 9/10).** Repeating bill features in every member JSON row wastes storage and makes schema drift harder to inspect. Decision: snapshot headers store canonical event features, event source revisions, and `event_sha256`; member rows store only canonical member/caucus features and combine their bytes with `event_sha256` when deriving `row_sha256`.

### Code-quality findings and decisions

- `etl/sources/congressGovMembers.ts:107` logs a URL containing `api_key`. Remove full-URL logging and log only source, Congress, page, and offset. Prior learning applied: `never-log-api-request-params`.
- State-name normalization is duplicated between current and historical sources. Move it to one pure ETL helper with fixtures for states and territories before another adapter is added.
- `etl/extractHouseVotes.ts:553` maps Senate session years only for Congress 119. Extract `congressSessionYears(congress)` and pure House/Senate list/detail parsers with injected `fetch`; keep the daily entry point as a thin caller.
- `computeMemberStats` is not a historical sanity engine: `etl/computeStats.ts:100-145` loads every vote, `:176-180` uses current party, and `:270-275` repeatedly filters the entire vote set. A1 uses set-based SQL joined to the effective term interval instead.
